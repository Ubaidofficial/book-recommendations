## Audience

Readers who choose books by who recommends them: people looking for what
founders, scientists, writers and other notable people actually read, and
readers who want a list with sources rather than bestseller rankings.

## Copy blocks

- **Name + tagline (≈60 chars):** BookMentions — book recommendations with sources
- **Short (≈160 chars):** Discover books recommended by notable people and curated lists. Each recommendation links to the interview, podcast or essay it came from where we have it.
- **Medium (≈300 chars):** BookMentions collects book recommendations from authors, scientists, founders and other notable people — from interviews, podcasts, essays and published reading lists. Books are ranked by how many distinct people recommend them, not by sales, and each recommendation links to its source where one exists.
- **Long (≈500 chars):** BookMentions is a free book-discovery site built on recommendations from notable people. It gathers public recommendations from interviews, podcasts, essays, shareholder letters and published reading lists, and links each one to its original source wherever it has one. Books rise by how many distinct people recommend them rather than by sales rank. Browse by person, by subject list (history, science, business, fiction and more) or by series, and report any recommendation you think is wrongly attributed.
- **Full description:**

  BookMentions answers a simple question: which books do the people you admire actually recommend, and where did they say it? It collects publicly available recommendations from interviews, articles, podcasts, social posts and curated reading lists, and records the source alongside each one.

  Books are ranked by the number and quality of recommendations, so widely endorsed books rise and lesser-known picks still surface. Readers can browse by person, by subject lists such as history, science, philosophy and fiction, or by series. The site does not generate or invent recommendations; some older entries do not have a source link yet, and readers can report anything that looks wrong.

## Key points

- Recommendations from notable people, linked to the original source where available
- Ranked by how many distinct people recommend a book, not by sales
- Browse by person, subject list or series
- Free, no sign-up needed (confirm)
- Errors can be reported and are reviewed
